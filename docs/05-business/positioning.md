# Positioning

Who we sell to first, what we say in one sentence, and why a café would pick us over what it uses now. Market research was done on 9 Nov 2026 from public sources; most local vendors publish little, so the gaps are marked and go into the field visits (`go-to-market.md`).

## Who we sell to first

Independent cafés and small restaurants in central Yerevan (Kentron) that:

- have 8–25 tables and table service (not a counter with a queue);
- run service on paper today: printed menus, waiters writing orders, slips carried to the kitchen;
- get Russian-speaking and foreign guests as well as locals;
- have an owner who is often on site and decides alone.

Not first: chains (Coffee House, 57 shops, runs Syrve through Eatery Club ([eatery.club](https://www.eatery.club/en/clients/coffee-house))), fine dining with its own POS, fast food counters, delivery-only kitchens.

Why these cafés:

- Tourists need the menu in Russian or English. Armenia had 2,263,642 tourist arrivals in 2025, up from 2,208,179 in 2024 ([ARKA](https://arka.am/en/news/tourism/nearly-140-000-tourists-visited-armenia-in-december-2025/)), and about 40 % come from Russia ([rustourismnews.com](https://www.rustourismnews.com/2026/07/16/armenia-welcomes-over-1-million-tourists-in-first-half-of-2026/)).
- Competition is tight. EVN Report counts about 1,500 bars and restaurants in Yerevan, against about 400 a little over a decade ago ([EVN Report, Sep 2024](https://evnreport.com/raw-unfiltered/the-life-cycle-of-a-yerevan-restaurant/)); Tripadvisor lists about 1,700 Yerevan restaurants ([Tripadvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurants-g293932-Yerevan.html); the count came through a search tool, the page blocks automated reading). Neither is an official count; we found none.
- One person decides, so a sale takes one visit and a free month, not a procurement process.

The market is small. 200 cafés at 15,000 ֏ is 3,000,000 ֏ a month, about $8,300. That pays a small team well; it isn't a reason to raise venture money on Armenia alone.

## The pitch

One sentence, for owners:

> Guests open your menu on their own phone in Armenian, Russian or English and order from the table; the kitchen sees it on a screen within seconds; you see the whole evening in Telegram. We set it up from a photo of your paper menu.

Positioning statement, for us:

> For owners of independent cafés in Yerevan who run service on paper, Pativ is the table ordering and kitchen system built in Armenian first, which goes live from a photo of the paper menu and keeps working when the café Wi-Fi doesn't.

## What cafés use today, and why they'd switch

| Today | What the owner likes about it | Where it fails | What we offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper menu | Free, nothing to learn | Reprint on every price change; tourists can't read it; waiters run back and forth; orders misheard | Menu in three languages from one edit; orders typed by the guest; sold-out dishes greyed out at once |
| PDF or photo of the menu on Instagram | Free, guests already follow the café | Unreadable on a phone, out of date, no ordering, no Russian or English unless typed by hand | Same link, a real menu with search in three scripts, and ordering |
| View-only QR menu (GisMenu, Bonee, OddMenu) | Cheap or free | Menu only: the order still goes through the waiter and paper | Ordering, kitchen screen, waiter app, Telegram, from the same QR |
| Full POS (iiko from 24,000 ֏ per cash desk, Mersoft, Norksys, 1C-based systems) | Bills, stock, HDM receipts, all in one | Costs more, needs a computer or terminal and an installer; built for restaurants with a manager | No hardware, live in 30 minutes, and at the proposed 15,000 ֏ well under iiko START. We don't replace their POS or HDM; we sit next to them |
| Delivery apps (Menu.am, Glovo, Yandex Eats) | Orders from outside | Not for guests in the hall | Not a competitor in Stage 1; Stage 3 adds the café's own takeaway page |

Where the others are honestly better: a full POS prints fiscal receipts and tracks stock, and Stage 1 does neither. Cafés that need that today should keep their POS. We tell them so.

### Fiscal receipts (HDM)

- Cafés must issue cash register (HDM) receipts, and the State Revenue Committee fines catering businesses for violations ([ARKA, 2024](https://arka.am/en/news/business/src_imposes_fines_worth_189_million_drams_for_violation_of_cash_register_/)).
- Law HO-309-N of 3 July 2026 lets the Government make electronic cash registers (e-HDM) mandatory by sector from 1 January 2027; the decision naming the sectors is expected in late December 2026. Restaurants aren't named in what we found ([lookuptax.com](https://lookuptax.com/tax-changes/armenia/ecrm-mandate-expansion-2027), secondary).
- Mersoft already prints HDM receipts from its software. We don't, and Stage 1 doesn't need to: the café keeps its register. If the December decision brings catering into e-HDM, Stage 2 needs a tax adviser's answer sooner (`reliability.md`, R19).

### How owners talk to each other

Among internet users in Armenia in 2021–22, 80 % used Viber, 62 % WhatsApp and 22 % Telegram; 40 % used Instagram ([Caucasus Barometer 2021: Viber](https://caucasusbarometer.org/en/cb2021am/MAPPVB/), [WhatsApp](https://caucasusbarometer.org/en/cb2021am/MAPPWTS/), [Telegram](https://caucasusbarometer.org/en/cb2021am/MAPPTL/), [Instagram](https://caucasusbarometer.org/en/cb2021am/APPINS/)). The figure is four years old and Telegram has likely grown since, but it's a warning: our alerts go to Telegram, and many owners may live in Viber.

Telegram stays the alert channel because its bot API is free. Viber charges €100 a month per new bot ([Viber](https://help.viber.com/hc/articles/15247629658525-Bot-commercial-model)), which the free-only rule excludes. For owners without Telegram, the admin sends the same alerts as web push notifications to their phone, which costs nothing (ADR-035). The support chat can run in whatever messenger the owner prefers; it's a person, not a bot.

## What this tells us

1. No one we found sells Armenian-first table ordering with a kitchen screen and Telegram alerts at a small-café price, with a public price list. The gap looks real, but desk research on a market this quiet is shallow. Twenty café visits will confirm it or not.
2. Menus alone are free on the market, so we charge for ordering and the kitchen, and give the menu away (`pricing.md`, option B).
3. iiko START at 24,000 ֏ per cash desk is the price owners will remember. We sit well under it and say why: no hardware, no installer, but also no fiscal receipts or stock.
4. A cheap regional product (QR Menyu, about 2,330 ֏) shows what undifferentiated QR ordering costs. Our reasons to pay more have to be visible in the first meeting: the owner's own menu in good Armenian, Russian and English on their phone, set up by someone who walks in.
5. Chains are taken. Independent cafés are where nothing is installed yet.

## What owners say on camera

From videos and interviews (`docs/00-research/video-notes.md`; quotes were rendered by Gemini from the audio and not checked against transcripts):

- iiko sells control, not ordering. The chairman of the Armenian Restaurant Association praises its P&L and round-the-clock human support ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U1l-QpXYwv8)). So our dashboard leads with average bill and best and weakest dishes, and support is a person on Telegram during service.
- Owners want to be away and hear about real problems only; a speaker cites an average lifespan of 13 months for catering businesses in Armenia ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cruLVUkTedU)). So alerts are exceptions by default (ADR-040) and the plan is month to month.
- Waiters resist tools that look like replacement; a hybrid where the guest chooses phone or waiter works ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ekg5IRRPELg)).
- Yerevan has a language gap between staff and guests: Russian-speaking waiters, Armenian-speaking guests, tourists ([EVN Report](https://evnreport.com/lifestyle/russian-speaking-waitstaff-stir-debate/)). Each person's screen speaks their language, whatever the guest ordered in.

## What we say, and to whom

| Person | What they care about | What we show |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Knowing what's happening without being there; fewer mistakes; tourists who order more | The Telegram feed of a real evening; the menu in three languages made from their photo |
| Manager or head waiter | Fewer runs, fewer arguments with the kitchen | The table map: who called, which food is ready |
| Cook | Slips that can be read from the pass, in Armenian | The kitchen screen from 2 metres |
| Waiter | Not being replaced; fewer trips; not needing the guest's language | The order pad and a tourist's English order arriving in the waiter's own language |

## What we don't claim

- "Cafés sell X % more": not until pilot data says so (`metrics.md`).
- "Five minutes from photo to menu": not until we've measured the AI on 15–20 real Yerevan menus (`gaps.md`).
- Fiscal receipts or replacing the cash register: Stage 1 does neither.
- "The only Armenian QR menu": GisMenu and Bonee exist.
