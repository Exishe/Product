# Yerevan studios publish prices, foreign agencies publish anchors

Yerevan web studios usually publish real prices. They give "from X AMD" figures (**«սկսած» / «-ից»**), sometimes as a full menu of packages, add-on unit prices and monthly retainers. Larger Armenian software houses give a single high "from 10,000 USD" figure or no price at all. US/EU and Eastern European agencies rarely publish a full price list for custom software. They publish a "Starts at $10K" anchor and a typical range, sell a paid **discovery step credited to the build**, and fix the price per milestone after scoping. Fixed packages abroad cover only narrow, repeatable work: store setup, audits, design sprints, bots and maintenance. SaaS products such as restaurant POS and QR menus use a different format again: **3–4 named tiers priced per month per location, per-device add-ons, a 15–33% annual discount and a "Contact sales" top tier**. The biggest gap in the Armenian market is that no studio page found prices **bots, CRM/ERP integrations or SaaS subscriptions as separate lines**. Only one studio (Makyan) prices **payment-gateway integration** separately: "from 200,000 AMD". Almost none state VAT or prepayment terms. All notes were gathered on 25 Nov 2026. In this report, **†** marks second-hand figures (a third-party blog, aggregator or vendor's content marketing, not the company's own price page). **‡** marks unverified figures (seen only in a search summary, the page could not be opened, or the data is stale or inconsistent). Unmarked figures were read on the company's own page.

## Two markets, two ways of showing a price

In Yerevan, publishing a full price list is a small-studio habit. Four presentation models dominate. The first and most common is **"from" prices per category**: Touch Web shows "Landing pages start at AMD 50,000", "Business websites start at AMD 200,000" and e-commerce "Projects start at AMD 450,000" ([Touch Web](https://www.touchweb.am/services/web-development)). The second is **named fixed packages with Armenian names**, not Start/Business/Pro. G Design Group sells Ստանդարտ / Պրեմիում / Էլ-կոմմերս at 80,000 / 150,000 / 250,000 դրամ, each with a build time in days ([G Design Group](https://www.gdesigngroup.net/service/kayqi-patrastum)). iMarketLab used six tiers, Պարզ / Հարմար / Շահավետ, each with a "+" upsell ([iMarketLab](https://imarketlab.am/promos/wordpress-kayqeri-patrastum/), ‡ a 2021 promo). The third is a **unit-price menu with hourly rates**. Makyan prices each feature, integration and extra language separately and charges "from 25,000 AMD per hour" for programming, with a minimum fee of 8,000 AMD for any job ([Makyan prices](https://www.makyan.com/en/page/prices/)). The fourth is **monthly maintenance tiers** (Makyan's Bronze to Platinum, covered below). Makyan also prices by the client's market: the same single-language site costs "from 290,000 AMD" for residents and "from 1,000 USD" for non-residents ([Makyan prices](https://www.makyan.com/en/page/prices/)). On the marketplace turn.am, **24 of 30 listings** on the first web-development page show a "Service price from" figure. The rest show nothing, and none uses a "price on request" label ([turn.am](https://turn.am/en/category/web-development)).

Abroad, the credible pattern is anchor, discovery and fixed-price proposal. RaftLabs writes "Starts at $10K" and "A typical MVP build runs $25,000 to $60,000", with the price "agreed in writing before the build" ([RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/services/minimum-viable-product-mvp-development)). Presta in Belgrade publishes a table of starting prices but says they are for self-qualification: "every quote is custom" ([Presta](https://wearepresta.com/pricing?view=full)). Discovery is sold as its own product. Geniusee prices it at Small $5,000 / Medium $10,000 / Large $15,000 ([Geniusee on Clutch](https://clutch.co/profile/geniusee/mobile-app-dev-package)). Presta charges "From 2,500 EUR", "Credited to the build". First Pier credits "every dollar" of a diagnostic toward a project signed within 90 days ([First Pier](https://www.firstpier.com/packages)). A smaller niche sells development as a subscription: Byteex charges $2,500/month for "Unlimited requests, one active at a time" and lets clients "pause or cancel at the end of any month" ([Byteex](https://byteex.co/services/custom-software)).

Directory and survey data put these anchors in context, but the sources disagree. On Clutch, web projects "typically cost less than $10,000", yet the average is **$66,499**. Mobile projects typically cost $10,000–$49,999, against an average of **$90,780**. Both pages put the average rate at $25–$49/h. Clutch's "average" figures are pulled up 5–10× by a few large projects, so they are not a sound base for a small studio's list.

## Wording, units and terms that repeat across pages

Armenian pages write prices as "80 000 դրամ" with «սկսած» or «-ից». They give timelines in days per package («Կայքի պատրաստումը 5 օր») and mark excluded features «չկա» in a comparison matrix ([G Design Group](https://www.gdesigngroup.net/service/kayqi-patrastum); [iMarketLab](https://imarketlab.am/promos/wordpress-kayqeri-patrastum/)). Makyan's standard disclaimer is "The exact price is determined following a free consultation", and complex design or extra functionality "can raise the price" ([Makyan](https://www.makyan.com/en/page/prices/)). turn.am listings show "Service avg. time". One states payment by "bank transfer, cash, and crypto" and "Signs a contract" ([turn.am](https://turn.am/en/service/razrabotka-saitov-dlia-malogo-biznesa-zaiavki-onlain-zapis-i-boty/detail)). **VAT (ԱԱՀ) and prepayment percentages appear on no Armenian page checked.** Makyan's "Prices include taxes" names no rate. A 50/50 prepayment is widely assumed but could not be cited. Discounts are shown openly: a portfolio discount, and seasonal banners such as «Բոլոր ծառայությունները մինչև −50%» ([WebStart](https://webstart.am/for-client/vorqan-arje-kayi-patrastume-hayastanum-2026/)).

Foreign pages always state the unit: "one-time", "/month", "total", "per hour", "Per 60 minutes", "per location", "per device", "per screen per month". Inclusions read "Everything in [lower tier], plus…". The standard disclaimers are "Final scope and cost depend on complexity, integrations, and timeline: every quote is custom" ([Presta](https://wearepresta.com/pricing?view=full)); "upon client request and will be estimated additionally" ([Geniusee](https://clutch.co/profile/geniusee/mobile-app-dev-package)); "Third-party usage costs, such as AI model tokens or paid APIs, are billed at cost on top" ([Byteex](https://byteex.co/services/custom-software)); and an explicit **"Excludes" list** per package ([GetDevDone](https://getdevdone.com/order-now/website-development)). Payment terms are worded by milestone, never by percentage. Examples: "Pay by milestone. You see progress before each invoice", with IP transferring "with each milestone payment" ([RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/services/minimum-viable-product-mvp-development)); a deposit secures the start date, and later payments are "tied to delivered phases, not the calendar" ([Presta](https://wearepresta.com/pricing?view=full)). CTAs split in two: "Order Now" / "Book this package" for fixed items, and "Get a Quote" / "Book a call" for custom work ([GetDevDone](https://getdevdone.com/order-now/website-development); [First Pier](https://www.firstpier.com/packages)). Trust lines such as "you own 100% of the code" appear next to the price ([RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/services/minimum-viable-product-mvp-development)).

## Practical options for structuring the price list

These options come from the precedents above. They are not mutually exclusive, and the choice stays with the owner.

| Option | What it looks like | Closest precedent | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. "From" price per category | One line per category: «Վեբ հավելված՝ սկսած X դրամ», «Բոտ՝ սկսած X դրամ», etc. | Touch Web, turn.am | Matches local habit and is quick to publish; gives the client little sense of scope |
| B. Fixed packages for repeatable items, "from" for custom work | 2–3 named packages (e.g. store, bot, payment connection) with timelines and «չկա» rows; custom apps and integrations stay "from X" | G Design Group, GetDevDone, Presta | The most transparent option; packages need firm scope caps (pages, screens, products, revisions) |
| C. Unit-price menu | Per-feature, per-gateway, per-integration and per-language prices, plus an hourly rate and a minimum fee | Makyan | Precise and good for add-ons; long, and can look like nickel-and-diming |
| D. Paid discovery credited to the build | A fixed "assessment" fee, credited if the client goes ahead | Presta, Geniusee, First Pier | Filters out tyre-kickers and pays for estimating; unusual locally, so it needs explaining |
| E. Integration = setup + monthly | One-time setup scaled by one-way/two-way and number of entities, plus a monthly monitoring fee and an hourly rate for out-of-scope work | Integrate IQ † | Clear scope units; recurring revenue; few local reference prices |
| F. Retainer ladder | 3–5 monthly tiers that differ by free edits, discount, hours or response time | Makyan (edits + discount), Freelock (hours + minimum term) | Recurring revenue; tier contents must be very concrete |
| G. SaaS tier table (for the studio's own products) | Free/Starter → "Popular" → Pro → "Contact us", per location per month, per-device add-ons, yearly toggle | Square, Poster, MENU TIGER | Familiar to café owners; needs a stated gate (locations, orders, menu items, devices) |

The precedents also leave several framing choices open. **Currency** can be AMD only, or AMD for residents and USD for non-residents (Makyan's split). **VAT** can be stated as included or excluded, which almost no local competitor does. **Third-party costs** (payment-provider commission, WhatsApp/Meta fees, iPaaS and hosting subscriptions) can go in a standing footnote "billed separately by the provider". **Payment terms** can be worded as deposit plus milestones or as a percentage split. **Warranty or free support** can be a fixed post-launch period (30/60 days, or 6/12 months by tier). **Source-code ownership** can be stated outright, or reserved for higher tiers as bot gigs do.

## Conclusion

The Yerevan market is open on simple sites and stores but leaves categories unpriced. No local page puts a public price on bots, CRM/ERP integrations, SaaS subscriptions or wallet integrations such as Idram and Telcell. VAT and prepayment terms are missing almost everywhere. Abroad, transparency comes from **scope caps and stated exclusions**, not from low prices. The most credible pages say exactly what a package excludes and which third-party fees sit on top. A studio can borrow that framing without borrowing foreign price levels.

Two caveats apply to the evidence. The Armenian payment and acquiring figures come mainly from one agency blog (†), and the international integration and bot prices come largely from vendor content and marketplaces (†/‡). Bank tariff pages, Idram/Telcell merchant terms and page 2 of turn.am were not checked. Neither were list.am, staff.am or social-media price posts. Treat these figures as presentation patterns and rough anchors, not as market rates.
